2026 Theme

Forge Forward: Scaling Our Industrial Foundation

Made in China 2025 — the industrial strategy that fueled China's rise — is now folded into its broader 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), touching nearly every major sector of its economy and pressing further into technological self-reliance. China has moved beyond chasing American innovation: its strategy centers on production, scale, and control of critical inputs, on the premise that controlling the lowest levels of the supply chain controls the capacity to scale up production. Meanwhile America's own manufacturing base has moved offshore, and our innovation capacity has suffered alongside it.

But America is a quick study. Congress is increasingly approving budget allocations to shore up our weakened capabilities in sourcing and processing raw materials, rare earths, and the lowest levels of the supply chain — yet public capital alone is insufficient. This year's conference places these issues at center stage, adding a Market Crafting layer that puts innovations in industrial policy at the heart of every discussion alongside the founders, investors, and experts building the next industrial base.

9:00–9:15 AMOpening Remarks

An overview of the event, a recap of the prior evening’s startup pitches and VC panel, and an introduction to the day’s industrial policy tabletop.

Ellen Chang, Michael Langman

9:15–9:45 AMOpening Fireside Chat: Dan Magy

A candid conversation on building defense technology for an era defined by speed, adaptability, and resilient supply chains, with Dan Magy, Founder & CEO of Firestorm.

Moderator: Heather Richman

9:45–10:30 AMOpening Capstone Panel: The U.S. vs. China and the Battle for Global Supply Supremacy

The global geopolitical landscape is defined by a fundamental clash of economic architectures. With the formal implementation of China’s 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), Beijing has pivoted from targeted sector subsidies to a system-wide, state-directed strategy designed to secure absolute technological self-reliance and command “new quality productive forces.” This panel contrasts the U.S. market-led, incentive-driven approach with China’s pervasive state-orchestrated model.

Moderator: Dan Shneiderman

10:30–10:45 AMBreak
10:45–11:30 AMCracks in the Supply Chain

Cracks in the global supply chain are getting harder to ignore — from critical materials and semiconductors to logistics bottlenecks and labor shortages. This panel explores the most urgent vulnerabilities and energizes a discussion on how data, re-shoring, dual-use manufacturing, and new public–private collaboration can build real resilience.

Moderator: Riley Thomas

11:30–12:15 PMAI Infrastructure in the Age of Great Power Competition — Compute, Energy, and Critical Minerals as Strategic Assets

AI has sparked a rapidly intensifying global economic competition over the infrastructure and power for the technology itself. This panel examines the emerging geopolitical and industrial dynamics shaping the AI infrastructure race.

Moderator: Andrew Stiles

12:15–1:15 PMLunch + Policy Tabletop Kickoff

A small working group from the conference gathers to spitball the day’s policy tabletop.

1:15–2:00 PMA Comparative Look at Industrial Policies Enabling Manufacturing — Asia, Europe, America

The U.S. and its allies are using different blends of public finance, industrial policy, and investment controls to strengthen domestic capabilities, reduce dependence on China, and gain long-term strategic advantage.

Moderator: Eric Lo

2:00–2:45 PMConcrete and Capital: Who Funds the Ground Underneath the Defense Industrial Base

Our manufacturing ambitions depend on funding infrastructure — the logistics, utilities, and buildings that make production possible. Yet the fastest-growing defense companies are often the ones least able to fund their physical expansion, and the ones that do are either paying for real estate with expensive capital or carrying it on balance sheet inefficiently. This panel examines where the chokepoints actually are and what institutional capital needs to see before it will own the physical layer rather than simply fund the company inside it. We must build the foundation that lets us build.

2:45–3:00 PMBreak
3:00–3:45 PMScaling Next-Gen Munitions

The modern battlefield demands high-volume, affordable precision to counter cheap drone swarms. This panel explores how defense startups scale next-generation munitions using commercial electronics and automotive-style mass production — manufacturing thousands of accurate, supersonic interceptors under $100,000, bypassing traditional supply chains to deploy affordable lethality at absolute scale.

Moderator: Dak Hardwick

3:45–4:15 PM$1T Private Capital for the National Interest — Policy Tabletop Outbrief

Crowding In Capital: Financing U.S. Reindustrialization Using America’s Greatest Instrument of Power — its Economic One. A discussion of the journey of crafting policies that incentivize deploying capital into longer-term, more capital-intensive sectors to secure our future.

Featuring: Jason Stack, Jake Higdon, Jen Cash, Ivan Shyr, Mark Choi

4:15 PMWrap-Up, then 4:30–6:00 PM Happy Hour
